# Assessment and mastery model

## Evidence, not reading
Reading completion is a separate boolean per learner/lesson. It never directly increases skill mastery. A new learner starts with zero evidence, displayed as “Not assessed” rather than assumed incompetence.

M1 objective grading uses exact server-side answer keys. Each exercise has three progressive hints and a detailed explanation. Hints consumed are stored on the server. Solutions become available after a correct submission or an explicit reveal; revealed answers cannot earn mastery. Repeat submissions do not produce unlimited mastery.

For each exercise, retain the first attempt and the best eligible attempt. Its evidence score is `0.7 × first score + 0.3 × best score`, multiplied by a hint factor `max(0.4, 1 − 0.2 × hints used before first success)`. An explicitly revealed solution contributes zero new evidence. A skill score is the average across all published practice exercises mapped to that skill, including unattempted ones as zero. This is a conservative initial coverage score, not a calibrated certification probability.

The original review queue repeats the same multiple-choice scenario after 1 day for incorrect work or 3 days for correct work. Subsequent successful reviews expand intervals (up to 30 days). It provides same-question practice and is not fresh-question retention evidence. M1 retains practice and review evidence separately; adding mastery recency/decay, difficulty weighting and exam evidence remains a versioned M2 change.

## Assessments
A pilot knowledge assessment validates the flow: begin, save answers, resume, submit atomically, show category scores and revisit recommendations. No hints or answer keys are sent during an active exam. Exam questions are distinct from practice questions. The pilot is not the full comprehensive exam.

Three additional Python knowledge evaluations are available: beginner (20 questions, five topics, approximately 35 minutes), intermediate (24 questions, six topics, approximately 55 minutes), and advanced (28 questions, seven topics, approximately 75 minutes). All are untimed and use code prediction, debugging and reasoning questions. The provisional learning target requires at least 80% overall and at least 60% in every topic, evaluated using exact fractions before display rounding. These thresholds are not calibrated certification standards. Results include correct/total counts, explanations, targeted official readings and a practical follow-up explicitly requiring separate manual review. Retakes reuse the same bank; exam results do not change mastery. See [Python levels and libraries](docs/PYTHON_LEVELS.md).

Attempts resume independently per learner and assessment. Starting an attempt freezes the complete definition, content version, answer key and policy in a server-only database snapshot. Saving, resuming and grading use that snapshot even after the published bank changes or is removed. Public active responses allowlist question fields and exclude answers, explanations and code-validation fixtures. The migration backfills original pilot attempts from a frozen historical definition without changing saved answers/results; unidentified legacy attempts fail closed. The advanced bank assumes intermediate fluency and covers protocols, typing, resource lifetime, async failure, concurrency, performance and packaging. Its advanced-specific practical-review rubric now connects to the composed manual project in ADR 0035. General and advanced fixed project references/manual reviews are supported in-app; tests/submissions do not certify expertise.

The three comprehensive exams now require different evidence: conceptual breadth; executable repair tests plus reviewer rubric; system-design artifacts plus staged incidents and reviewer rubric. Self-reported completion or LLM judgment alone must never certify practical or senior mastery.

Python content revision `2026.09.20-v2` simplifies all 72 answer explanations without changing questions, answer keys or scoring. New attempts capture this revision; existing snapshots and submitted results keep their original wording. The tests sample the [planned learning path](docs/PYTHON_LEARNING_PATH.md), not a complete expert curriculum.

## Required safeguards
- The twenty-six local Python projects have public local acceptance tests and self-reported checklists. Learners can submit repository references, design/evidence notes and reported test command/output; the app does not execute or independently verify those results or award practical mastery. Maintained references pass the tests; learners still need review and their own regression cases. The typed inbox also has static-check evidence, which cannot prove runtime behavior.
- All grading on the server; validate answer membership.
- User-scoped attempts, notes, reviews and exam sessions.
- Repeated exam submission returns the existing result.
- Submitted exam answers are immutable.
- No keyword-based grading presented as semantic understanding.
- Readiness uses declared skill coverage, not invented progress.

## Planning guidance
The personal plan uses the latest submitted result per assessment to recommend related teaching, with exact category fractions below 80% flagged for reinforcement. Active exams are excluded. Suggested placement never completes lessons or changes mastery; learners can choose the beginning instead. Correct practice after a weak result can clear its suggested task, including assisted practice, without certifying competence. Reading remains separate from practice. See ADR 0011 for the complete planning policy.

Plan policy `2026.09-plan-v2` also prioritizes due short-answer checks and resumes active drafts within the goal route/budget. During that queue calculation they replace duplicate scenario tasks for the topic. Historical recognition scores are unchanged.

## Mistake-driven recall
Version `2026.10-recall-v26` provides 390 exact short-answer questions across all 195 published lesson topics. Two or more recorded wrong answers flag repeated errors; the UI shows distinct original-question counts and calls this a review signal, not a diagnosis. Each active check pins its question/key/policy privately and saves the learner's draft. Fixed feedback exposes the answer and a simple explanation only after submission.

Original multiple-choice recognition, immediate practice, fresh delayed checks, repeated delayed questions and assisted work have separate correct/total counts. Delayed checks require a due topic and at least 24 hours since its latest recorded exposure. Fresh means the question ID has never been started by that account. Viewed/discarded questions remain seen; exhausted banks yield labelled repeats without fresh credit. Immediate practice never becomes delayed evidence by waiting before submission.

Help use is self-reported; an original scenario attempt during a check also marks it assisted. External help and lesson reading cannot be reliably detected. Correct independent fresh delayed work grows intervals from three days by doubling up to 30; repeated success retains the interval, while wrong/assisted delayed work resets it to one day. New mistakes can bring a long schedule forward, always respecting the recorded-exposure gap. This is a provisional practice policy and limited evidence, not calibrated memory measurement or certification. No recall result changes mastery, exams, reading, coding or project state. See ADR 0014.

## Project submissions and manual judgment
All twenty-nine current projects have four authored review criteria and a shared 0–3 evidence scale. Draft creation freezes the complete rubric; submitted artifacts, rubric snapshots and feedback cannot be edited. New versions reference their predecessor and require a change summary. Reported tests, self-review and invited-account human review are labelled separately.

Every criterion requires a strict integer score and supporting observations. A manual review records a summary and next actions. All criteria rated at least 2 produce “Meets stated rubric”; otherwise the outcome is “Changes requested”. These are attributable judgments against stated expectations, not automated implementation grades, calibrated readiness scores or certification. Reviewer qualifications and real-world identity are not verified. Each account can record one immutable review per version. Neither submission nor review changes skill mastery, lessons, exam results or project checklist state.
